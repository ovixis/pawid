Supporting text from the disease guidelines:

- FPV, ABCD-FPV, section "Primary vaccination course": "Three doses – one at 8 to 9 weeks of age, a second 3 to 4 weeks later, and a third 3 to 4 weeks later (at a minimum of 16 weeks of age) should be administered." and "After the kitten primary vaccination course, all cats should receive an additional vaccine dose at 10 to 16 months of age; this will ensure adequate vaccine-induced immunity for cats that did not adequately respond to the primary course."
- FPV safety, ABCD-FPV: "MLV FPV vaccines should never be administered to kittens under 4 weeks of age for the same reason: to avoid damage to the cerebellum, which is still developing in young neonates".
- FCV, ABCD-FCV, "Primary vaccination course": "the primary course of vaccination is usually started at around nine weeks of age, although some vaccines are licensed for use at an earlier age. Kittens should receive a second vaccination two to four weeks later, but not earlier than at 12 weeks of age, followed by a third vaccination at the age of 16 weeks. [...] The final dose of the primary vaccination course is given at 10 to 16 months of age."
- FHV, ABCD-FHV, "Primary vaccination course": "the primary course of vaccination is therefore usually started at around nine weeks of age, although some products are licensed for earlier use, if the infection risk is high. Kittens should receive a second vaccination two to four weeks later, with the second given not less than twelve weeks of age. A first booster is recommended at the age of 10 to 16 months."
- FeLV, ABCD-FeLV, "Primary vaccination course": "Kittens at risk should be vaccinated at the age of eight or nine weeks and twelve weeks, together with the core vaccinations. The primary vaccination course includes an additional vaccine dose given one year later."
- Rabies, ABCD-RAB, "Primary vaccination course": "Kittens should be vaccinated not earlier than at 12 to 16 weeks of age to avoid interference from maternal antibodies, with revaccination one year later (depending on data sheet recommendations for each brand of vaccine). [...] However, national or regional legislation regarding vaccination type and interval should be adhered to."
- C. felis, ABCD-CF, "Vaccination": "Vaccination of kittens generally begins at 8-9 weeks of age with a second injection 3-4 weeks later at around 12 weeks of age. [...] Annual boosters are recommended for cats that are at continued risk of exposure to infection."
- B. bronchiseptica, ABCD-BB, "Primary vaccination course": "The modified live vaccine is licensed for use as a single vaccination with annual boosters." Safety: "Bb vaccines containing viable bacteria should never be administered to kittens less than 4 weeks of age as recommended by the manufacturer."
- General, ABCD-MDA: "It is common practice to perform the first core vaccination at 8-9 weeks of age (or earlier in kittens at special risk or in rescue shelters), and to give additional doses at 2- to 4-week intervals until the age of 12-16 weeks or older (e.g. for FPV)".

**About the 26-week / 6-month dose:** ABCD does not use it. The ABCD tool and the FPV, FCV and FHV guidelines put the last primary dose at **10 to 16 months**. The only extra kitten dose ABCD mentions is an optional **20-week** FPV dose ("in regularly vaccinated breeding queens, also consider giving a 20-week vaccination", ABCD-TOOL p. 2). A 26-week dose appears in WSAVA guidance, which is outside the scope of these notes.

### 1.4 Lapsed or unknown history (Protocol 5)

ABCD-TOOL p. 5 ("Adult cat: special cases. Lapsed vaccinations, initial adult cat vaccinations or cats with an unknown vaccination history", for a cat "Vaccinated over 3 years ago" or "Never vaccinated or unknown vaccination history"): FCV/FHV given as two doses, then "2-4 weeks later" and "One year later". FPV: "* FPV antibody testing should be performed: only vaccinate if indicated. As FPV is a potent MLV vaccine, only one vaccination is required in adult cats, without a repeat 2-4 weeks later." Also "Annual health check for risk profile analysis".
ABCD-FHV: "If booster vaccinations have lapsed, a single injection is adequate if the interval since the last vaccination is less than three years; if the interval is greater than three years, it is recommended that two injections, 2-4 weeks apart, should be administered."

### 2.4 Rabies status note (STIKO-RAB26, 2026)

German: "Seit 2022 wird die Tollwutimpfung in der Leitlinie zur Impfung von Kleintieren nicht mehr als Core-Impfung geführt. [...] Sie wird daher weiterhin für Hunde empfohlen, die einem Expositionsrisiko ausgesetzt sind. Sie ist ohnehin für Hunde, Katzen und Frettchen vorgeschrieben, die auf grenzüberschreitende Reisen mitgeführt werden sollen. Innerhalb der EU und aus gelisteten Drittländern kann dies frühestens mit Vollendung der 15. Lebenswoche des Tieres erfolgen."
Translation: "Since 2022, the small-animal guideline no longer lists rabies vaccination as core. [...] It is still recommended for dogs exposed to a risk. It is mandatory anyway for dogs, cats and ferrets taken on cross-border journeys. Within the EU and from listed third countries, such travel is possible at the earliest once the animal has completed its 15th week of life."
The same news item says that the 2026 imported rabies case in Rheinland-Pfalz does **not** change that assessment.

## 3. Other Europe-wide body for DOGS (e.g. FECAVA)

**Verified:** I found **no** standalone European dog (or cat) vaccination schedule published by FECAVA. FECAVA points readers to other groups' guidelines:
- FECAVA-1 (2020-04-22, written by the chairman of the WSAVA Vaccination Guidelines Group): "the latest advice provided to the profession by vaccination guidelines groups (e.g. the WSAVA or ABCD guidelines in Europe)" and "Every practice should base their vaccination protocols on the latest scientific advice and keep these under regular review as guidelines are updated periodically."
- FECAVA-S: the site search result "Feline Vaccination Guidelines" lists "2020 AAHA/AAFP Feline Vaccination Guidelines ABCD Vaccine Recommendations for Cats WSAVA Vaccination Guidelines". All three are external, and two are out of scope here.

**Result:** at European level there is a cat body (ABCD) but no equivalent dog body with its own schedule. For dogs, the only in-scope official source found is national: StIKo Vet (Germany). Other national bodies (e.g. Austria, Switzerland, France, Italy, UK BSAVA) were **not** researched in this pass.

## 4. EU rabies rule: Regulation (EU) No 576/2013

URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32013R0576

| Item | Quote | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Rabies vaccination needed for movement between Member States | "they have received an anti-rabies vaccination that complies with the validity requirements set out in Annex III;" | Article 6(b) (also Article 10(1)(b) for entry from territories/third countries) |
| Minimum age 12 weeks | "the pet animal was at least 12 weeks old at the date on which the vaccine was administered;" | Annex III, point 2(b) |
| Valid from ≥21 days after primary protocol | "the period of validity of the vaccination starts from the establishment of protective immunity, which shall not be less than 21 days from the completion of the vaccination protocol required by the manufacturer for the primary vaccination, and continues until the end of the period of protective immunity, as prescribed in the technical specification of the marketing authorisation" | Annex III, point 2(e) |
| Lapsed booster = new primary | "a revaccination must be considered a primary vaccination if it was not carried out within the period of validity referred to in point (e) of the previous vaccination." | Annex III, point 2(f) |
| Vaccine must be after microchip | "the date of administration referred to in point (c) does not precede the date of application of the transponder or tattoo or the date of reading of the transponder or the tattoo" | Annex III, point 2(d) |
| Derogation for young animals (Member State option) | "either less than 12 weeks old and have not received an anti-rabies vaccination; or between 12 and 16 weeks old and have received an anti-rabies vaccination, but do not yet meet the validity requirements referred to in point 2(e) of Annex III." | Article 7(1) (Article 11 for third-country entry) |

Note: the 12-week rule is **Annex III point 2(b)**, which Articles 6(b) and 10(1)(b) apply. Articles 7 and 11 let Member States make exceptions for young animals.

## 6. Inference / unclear (NOT verified; do not encode as fact)

1. **StIKo Vet PDF page numbers and wording:** not read (bot check). The stiko-vet.fli.de Impftabelle pages link to the 2025 PDF and look like the online version of the same guideline, but I did not compare them line by line. **Unclear** until someone opens the PDF by hand.
2. **Rabies non-core since when:** STIKO-RAB26 says "Seit 2022" in the guideline and "Einschätzung der StIKo Vet von 2021". The exact edition date is not verified here.
3. **"15 Lebensmonate" vs ABCD "10-16 months":** the two sources differ. A timeline built from birth date must show which source each dose comes from, not merge them.
4. **ABCD FeLV adult interval:** the guideline's summary paragraph says "every two to three years for cats over three to four years", while its "Revaccinations" sentence and the 2025 tool say "3 years and older: boost every 3 years". The tool and that sentence are used above; the inconsistency is recorded here.
5. **ABCD rabies core status:** the ABCD-TOOL puts rabies under "non core" with "Can be essential, depending on local prevalence, legal requirements". The ABCD rabies guideline itself does not use the word "core" in what I extracted. Treat it as non-core plus legally required where law says so.
6. **ABCD Protocol 2 (shelter) non-core columns:** text layer scrambled, page not checked visually. **Unclear.**
7. **StIKo Vet dog HCC ages:** none given, because it is non-core. Combination products will usually include CAV-2 at the core visits, but that is an inference about products, not a recommendation.
8. **"15. Lebenswoche" for rabies travel** (STIKO-RAB26) is consistent with 12 weeks + 21 days under EU-576. That arithmetic is an inference; the regulation's text is "at least 12 weeks" plus "not less than 21 days".
9. **Reg. 576/2013 still in force on 2026-11-22:** I fetched the original act (CELEX 32013R0576) and did not check consolidated versions or later amendments. Verify on EUR-Lex before release.
10. **Other European national bodies** (AT, CH, FR, IT, NL, UK, Nordic) were not researched.
